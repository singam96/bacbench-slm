🧬 E. coli Gene Function Classification — Model Comparison

Dataset: BacBench antibiotic resistance protein sequences · E. coli (TaxID 562)

Task: multi-class classification of top 15 gene products

1. Objective

Classify E. coli protein sequences into functional categories (e.g. “DNA-binding protein”, “transporter”) using four model families: a Transformer, a BiLSTM, a 1D CNN, and a k-mer feature-based Random Forest. All deep-learning models share the same tokenizer, vocabulary, dataset split and training recipe so the comparison is fair.

2. Models Compared

1. Transformer (ProteinCausalLM)

Causal Transformer encoder adapted for classification. 6 layers, 8 heads, d_model=256, GELU activation with pre-LayerNorm. Mean pooling of the encoder outputs followed by a linear classifier. Captures long-range dependencies in protein sequences.

2. BiLSTM (ProteinBiLSTM)

Bidirectional LSTM with 6 layers and hidden size 256. Processes sequences in both directions, then mean-pools the hidden states and classifies. Efficient for sequence modelling with good local-context awareness.

3. CNN (ProteinCNN)

1D convolutional neural network with 4 parallel filters (kernel sizes 3, 5, 7, 9), each followed by ReLU and global max-pooling. The multi-scale features are concatenated and fed to a linear classifier. Fast to train and captures local k-mer-like sequence motifs.

4. Random Forest

Classical ML baseline. Features are normalised 1-mer, 2-mer and 3-mer frequency vectors plus log sequence length (sparse DictVectorizer). An ensemble of 300 decision trees with class-weight balancing. Provides an interpretable, non-neural reference point.
